    Your privacy on The Wheel

    The Wheel lists what is on, finds people for the shifts that need filling, and keeps who you are to you.

    What holds today

    • No phone number. The Wheel asks for no phone number to look, to add a listing, or to help. A number is your legal name in another form.
    • The organiser keeps the door. Listings link to the organiser's own page when there is one. Tickets and shift sign-ups happen there, with them.
    • Practitioners choose who sees each detail. Every line of a practitioner’s profile is set to everyone, members or held back. Contact details go to members unless the practitioner opens them.
    • Shifts and asks stay between the people in them. Everyone sees how many took a shift; the organiser holding the listing sees the pen names. An ask’s thread is seen by its two sides only.
    • Vouches are proofs. A member vouches for a practitioner with the same kind of proof as a check-in: one per member for each practitioner. The Wheel counts vouches; who vouched stays with them.
    • Monero tickets go straight to the organiser. You pay the organiser's own address. The Wheel checks the payment from its transaction ID and key, then keeps a fingerprint of each ticket code and a fingerprint of the payment's ID, so one payment buys once. The ID and key go once the payment settles. Your codes come from a token kept on your device, with no account and no address of yours.
    • A person checks every listing. Each one waits for a reviewer before it shows as checked, and links to where it was found.
    • No ads, no analytics. The Wheel carries no advertising and no tracking code of its own. What it knows about anyone stays off the market.
    • Phone-free accounts. Joining makes a key on your device and gives you a pen name. Adding and reviewing listings are tied to that pen name, so spam has a cost.
    • Proof in place of a list. Members prove they belong, and that they turned up, with zero-knowledge proofs: The Wheel learns that a member checked in, and which member stays with them. The proof protects more as more people join; the Roadmap shows the count.
    • Reputation that rewards the long run, earned by listings and reviews that hold up, set after a study of how it can be gamed. How rep works.
    • Group chats kept to members. A group's invite link goes to signed-in members, with the organiser's OK, and senders' names and numbers come out of a pasted announcement before it is read. Joining group chats.
    • Links and feeds. A shared link shows a preview in the chat it lands in: the chat app reads the listing's name, date and place from The Wheel. Calendar and feed apps ask for the whole list, the same list for everyone who follows it. The weekly digest comes from our own push server, ntfy.aureliusxradix.com, which sees the phones subscribed to it and holds each week's message for two days.
    • Kept on your device. Reading settings, favourite kinds and, once installed as an app, a copy of the page and the last list stay in your browser.
    • Reports. Reviewers read a report's reason and note; the pen name that sent it stays on the server.
    • Organiser accounts stand apart. An organiser account is its own key with a public name. It travels separately from your pen name, so the two stay unlinked.
    • An open record. The moderation log lists every check, fix and steward decision by role. Appeals go to fresh reviewers, or to the stewards for a rank reset.
    • Where it runs. Listings and accounts live on a machine we own and look after. The page reaches you through Cloudflare, and a post or flyer you add is read by Anthropic's Claude to turn it into a listing.

    Live now

    • One list, three modes. Day for workshops, skillshares and volunteer shifts. Night for gigs and evening gatherings. Recovery for retreats, sound baths and rest.
    • Listings people add. Paste a post or a link, or drop in a flyer. The Wheel reads it into a listing, and members check it before it shows as checked.

    How rep works

    Rep is your standing on The Wheel. It grows when what you add and what you check holds up, and it opens more of the tools. Rep is earned here, by what you do.

    What earns it

    • A listing you added passes review: 2 points. One that reviewers turn down takes 2 away.
    • Your review agrees with how a listing settles: 1 point. A review on the other side takes 1 away.
    • An organiser thanks you for a shift: 2 points, for up to 3 shifts a week. Thanks count toward Regular; Reviewer stays with listings and reviews, so review weight comes from checking.
    • A weekly limit: points count for up to 5 listings and 5 reviews a week, so steady use over months counts for more than a busy day.
    • Points arrive once a listing settles, for the person who added it and for every review on it.

    How a listing gets checked

    Each review counts by the reviewer's tier at the moment they cast it. The reviews for a listing add up like this: two Regulars together reach 64%, one Reviewer alone reaches 70%. New members' reviews add up to 50% at most, however many there are, so every listing needs a Regular or above among its reviews to settle. At 70% sure, the listing settles as checked, or as turned down when the reviews go the other way.

    Fair play

    • You review other people's listings. Your own go to someone else.
    • One key, one person. A pen name keeps its key for life, and whoever hands you a key still holds it, so a given account stays shared with the giver.
    • A ban resets rank to zero. Your past reviews stay, so other listings keep their standing. You can ask the stewards to look again from your Me page.
    • A turned-down listing gets one more look. Whoever added it can send it back to review once, to members who have yet to review it.
    • Everything is on the record. The moderation log shows every check, fix and steward decision, by role.
    • Turning up to events: check-ins stay apart from pen names by design. Counting them toward rep is on the Roadmap, once check-ins are hard to fake.

    Version 1. The numbers will change as The Wheel learns what works. Your rep and tier are on the Me page.

    Joining group chats

    Some gatherings are organised in a group chat. The Wheel lists the gathering and, when the organiser shares it openly, the link to the group, for signed-in members. The group belongs to its organiser and runs on its app's terms: what you share there stays between you, the people in it and the app.

    Before you join any group

    • Everyone in a group sees your profile name and photo. A first name or a nickname works, and a photo of something other than your face.
    • Anything you post can be screenshotted and passed on. Keep where you live, work and travel for people you know.
    • A group link opens the group to whoever holds it. Pass it on the way the organiser asks.
    • Leave a group once the gathering is over, unless you want to stay for the next one.

    Signal

    Signal shows your phone number only to people who already have it saved. To check: Settings, Privacy, Phone number. "Who can see my number" is set to "Nobody" by default. Set "Who can find me by my number" to "Nobody" as well if you'd like people to reach you by a username only, and pick a username in your profile.

    WhatsApp

    Everyone in a WhatsApp group sees your phone number. WhatsApp is rolling out usernames in stages through 2026: when your app has Settings, Account, Username, set one, and people see the username in place of your number unless they already have it saved. Until then, a second number (a prepaid SIM) keeps your own number to yourself.

    Telegram

    Telegram shows your number only to people you've saved as contacts, and you can post in groups with your number hidden. For more: Settings, Privacy and Security, Phone Number, and set "Who can see my phone number" to "Nobody". A username lets people reach you by name.

    Discord

    Discord shows your display name; your phone number and email stay with Discord. To keep strangers in a server from messaging you: User Settings, Content & Social, and turn off direct messages from server members. You can apply it to every server, or set it per server in that server's privacy settings.

    Adding a listing from a group chat

    • Paste the announcement and tick "It's from a group chat". The Wheel takes out senders' names, phone numbers, emails, @handles and chat links before it reads it.
    • Add the group's link only when the organiser shares it openly: on a flyer, in a public post, or by asking for it to be passed on. Signed-in members see it; everyone else sees that the gathering has a group.
    • Links to one person stay out: wa.me and signal.me links, and Telegram usernames.
    • Check the cleaned announcement before you send it. Reviewers check the listing against it, so take out anything else that names a person.

    Checked 21 October 2026 against Signal's and Telegram's own pages, Discord's help centre and reports of WhatsApp's usernames. Apps change their settings: the app's own help has the latest.

    For organisers

    Run a circle, a gig night or a beach clean? An organiser account lets you keep your listings right, post updates, sell tickets paid in Monero straight to your own wallet, and open check-ins with a door code that changes every minute. It's free.

    How it works, in six steps
    1. Make the account. The name people know your group by, and your page: your own site, or your page on Facebook or Instagram. The account gets a key of its own. Keep a copy in a password manager; several people running one group can share it.
    2. Prove the page is yours. Put your code (it looks like WHEEL-7K2QMP) anywhere on that page: the about text, a post, a pinned comment. Then tap Check my page. Facebook and Instagram often turn outside readers away; when the check comes back empty, ask a steward, who confirms it with you another way, often in person. Once you're proven, the code can come down.
    3. Claim your listings. Find each one and tap Claim. A listing that links to your page is yours straight away; others go to a steward first. A listing still to come onto The Wheel: add it under + Add a listing, then claim it.
    4. Keep them right. Change the date, time, place, price, tickets or status, and post an update everyone sees on the listing: moved, sold out, bring a plate. Your changes show at once.
    5. Sell tickets, if you like. Under Monero tickets on your listing, add your own Monero address, a price in NZ$ and how many. Buyers pay you directly; their wallet's proof of payment comes to The Wheel, which checks it against your address and gives them their codes. The whole amount lands in your wallet and stays yours.
    6. At the door. Open check-in and your phone shows a door code that changes every minute. Members type it into the listing's "I'm here". The Wheel counts how many came in; which members they were stays with each of them. Ticket codes get checked on the same screen, each one once.

    Practitioners and places

    The same account lists a practice in Recovery: healers, bodyworkers, counsellors. Under As a practitioner, each line of your profile has its own setting, everyone, members or held back, and contact can go through a private messenger like Signal. Run a sauna, hot pools or a float centre? Under Places you look after, claim it and keep its hours right.

    Selling elsewhere too

    Tickets on Gather, Eventfinda, Flicket, iTicket, Humanitix or another seller: put the link in your listing's Tickets field and the card names the seller, "Tickets on Gather", linking straight there.

    Who sees what

    • Everyone sees your organiser name and page, "Kept up to date by" on your listings, a public page with your listings, and your updates in the moderation log.
    • Stays with you: your own pen name, and whether you're a member at all. The organiser key travels on its own, apart from any member login, so the two stay unlinked.
    • Your listings still sit in the open list. Members can report a problem, and reviews and the moderation log work for them as for any listing.

    Moderation log

    Every check, fix, merge and steward decision, newest first, written where anyone can read it. Each line names the role that acted; pen names stay with their members. A turned-down listing shows as "a listing".
